# Entering your first records

The app starts empty. Twenty minutes in this order gets every page working.
Nothing here needs exact numbers: a balance can be corrected tomorrow, and
transactions can be edited or deleted.

1. **Accounts.** Add each place your money lives or is owed: checking,
   savings, each credit card, loans, retirement, brokerage, cash. Pick the
   type; cards and loans are marked as liabilities for you. The name is
   what you'll see everywhere, so keep it short ("Chase checking").
2. **Net worth → Update balances.** Enter today's balance for every
   account, as the bank shows it (what you owe on a card is a positive
   number). Save them all at once. Net worth appears on Home straight away;
   the chart starts once a second month is in. Do this around the same day
   each month; Home reminds you after 30 days.
3. **Budgets → Categories.** Add your spending categories (Groceries,
   Rent, Eating out, Transport, Fun…) and one or two income ones (Pay).
   Six to ten is plenty to start. Then, under **Budget**, open **Set
   budgets** and type this month's amount for each; leave one blank for no
   budget. Next month, **Copy last month** carries them over.
4. **Bills.** Add the bills and subscriptions that come round: amount, how
   often, the next date each is due, and the account it's paid from. Tick
   **Autopay** for the ones that pay themselves. When one goes out, press
   **Mark paid**: the date moves on and, with the box ticked, the payment is
   logged as a transaction.
5. **Transactions.** Two ways to fill them:
   - **Import** (fastest): on your bank's or card's website, export the
     last month or two as a CSV, then on the Import page pick the account,
     drop the file in, check the preview and press **Import**. If a card
     statement lists charges as positive numbers, tick **Spending is
     positive**. Importing an overlapping file later is safe: the same row
     never goes in twice.
   Where the CSV button is on each card's website (menus move, but roughly):

   | Card | Where | Notes |
   |---|---|---|
   | Capital One | the account → View Transactions → Download → CSV | debit and credit columns; nothing to tick |
   | Discover | Activity & Statements → Download Transactions → CSV | charges are positive; the flip box ticks itself |
   | Amex | Statements & Activity → Download → CSV, with all details | charges are positive; the flip box ticks itself |
   | Chase | the account → the download icon above the activity → CSV | charges are negative; the Memo column becomes notes |

   On a card, a payment row ("PAYMENT - THANK YOU") imports as a **transfer**,
   not income, and a refund stays as spending with a positive sign.
   - **By hand**: **Add transaction** on Home or Transactions. Type the
     amount as a positive number; the kind (income, expense, transfer)
     decides the sign.
   Afterwards, give imported rows a category on the Transactions page
   (pick one in the dropdown, change the category, save). Only categorised
   spending counts against a budget.
6. **Goals.** Name what you're saving for, the target and the date, and
   the account that holds the money; progress follows that account's
   balance.

Then **Home** shows net worth, this month's spending against its budget, the
savings rate, and what needs attention; **Stats** fills in as the months go
by.

## Habits that keep it honest

- Once a month: update every balance (Home nudges you), copy or adjust the
  budget, import the month's bank files, mark bills paid.
- Transfers between your own accounts (paying a card from checking, moving
  money to savings) are **transfers**, not spending: they're left out of
  the budget and the savings rate.
- A closed account is **closed** on the Accounts page, not deleted, so its
  history stays in the charts.
